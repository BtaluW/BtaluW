Five templates shipped, and a cache that did not know they existed

Registered five named resume templates and retired the old generic one, to clear a hard requirement: at least five templates rated ATS-friendly. Building them surfaced a real bug: applying a new template did not invalidate the public share-link's cached PDF, so a candidate switching templates could keep serving their old design to anyone with the link, indefinitely.

27 Aug 2026
week 35

A PDF that was 3.5% smaller than it should have been, silently

A banner résumé template pulled its header toward the page edge with a negative margin — looked right in the browser canvas. In the exported PDF, Chromium's print engine clipped the bled content entirely and silently shrank the whole document to fit, with no error. Fixed by shrinking the page's own padding instead of fighting it with a negative margin.

Message requests: two inbox tabs, not three

The written design put pending message requests in their own tab, mirroring the Instagram/LinkedIn pattern. 8 Jul 2026
week 28

A <label> around a dropdown breaks focus

Several profile-editor dropdowns kept jumping back to an earlier field after picking a value. Root cause: a <label> is meant for exactly one control, but these components render extra buttons inside once opened, the browser was forwarding focus to the first one it found. Fixed with a plain wrapper for any field that isn't a bare input.

GDPR erasure is not a DELETE statement

Candidates can erase their data; employers must retain some of it. Landed on a 30-day window that anonymizes retained records in place, so foreign keys survive and the person does not. The hardest part was accepting that the naive answer was wrong.

25 May 2026
week 22

Job postings and applying, shipped the same day

Job posting CRUD for recruiting orgs and the apply flow for candidates landed together — a posting needs a lifecycle (draft/published/closed) and a recruiting-staff-only write path before a single candidate ever sees it. The one rule that had to be right from day one: "one application per job per candidate" is a DB unique index, not a Rails validation — a validator alone races under concurrent submits.
